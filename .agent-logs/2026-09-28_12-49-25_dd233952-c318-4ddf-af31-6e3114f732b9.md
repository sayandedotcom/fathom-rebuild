It has 7 tasks:
1. **Clip logic:** the rules for how far a highlight reaches, clip length limits, matching Claude's labels to clips, and share tokens, all with unit tests.
2. **Database:** a new `clips` table and migration, plus freeing up clips whose cut got stuck.
3. **Cutting and the clip API:** cutting audio with ffmpeg, a script to test a cut locally, and routes to create, delete and retry clips. Deleting a meeting also deletes its clips' audio files.
4. **Highlights in processing:** after transcription, each highlight gets its time range and a label from Claude, and every clip is cut. The browser recorder's highlights are sent when the meeting is created.
5. **Meeting page and recorder:**
   - a Highlight button while the bot is recording, and one in the browser recorder
   - making a clip by selecting transcript lines
   - markers under the player and a colored band on clipped transcript lines
   - a Highlights section in the summary and a clip list with copy link, retry and delete
6. **Share page:** the public `/c/<token>` page. The site's top nav moves out of the shared layout so it doesn't appear there, but existing page addresses don't change.
7. **Wrap-up:** README, deploy, and checks on the live site.

Choices I made that aren't in the spec:
- **Snapping limit:** a highlight's edges snap to whole transcript lines, but never by more than 15 seconds. Otherwise one long monologue could turn a highlight into a multi-minute clip.
- **Label matching:** Claude gets each clip's start time and labels are matched back to the second, so `1:02` and `01:02` both work.
- **When highlights get placed:** during summarizing rather than right after transcription, so a retried or re-summarized meeting still places them.
- **Retries:** clips whose cut failed are tried again on every later summary.

I read from the git log that the speakers/templates plan is committed through the delete feature. Its last task (README and deploy) seems not done yet; the plan's final task can cover both.

Please review the plan. Which way should we run it?

- **Subagent-driven:** a fresh agent builds each task and a separate reviewer checks it before the next one starts, then the whole branch gets a final review. More thorough, but each task and review starts from scratch, so it costs more.
